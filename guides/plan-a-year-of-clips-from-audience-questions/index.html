How to plan a year of video clips from the questions your audience asks

By CliprAuto · Updated 2026-11-01

Short answer: pick one specialty, split it into twelve topics, collect real questions people type into search for each topic, and then look in your recordings for the moments that answer them. Questions you have not answered yet become topics for future episodes.

1. One specialty

One clear area you want to be known for. A narrow focus makes it easier for people and search engines to understand what you are about.

2. Twelve topics

Break the specialty into twelve topics, one per month if you like.

3. Real questions for each topic

Type each topic into a search box and note the suggestions that appear. They show what people actually ask, for example: "how long should podcast clips be", "turn podcast into shorts" or "how to add captions to short videos". Rewrite the best ones as questions. Thirty per topic is plenty.

4. Match questions to your recordings

Search your transcripts for the moments that answer each question. A good match is a clip that answers one question clearly. This is the step that matters most, because a long list of questions does not mean your recordings already answer them.

5. Fill the gaps

Questions with no good answer in your recordings are a gift: they are topics for your next episodes.

Questions and answers

Where do I find what my audience asks?

Search suggestions, comments on your videos, replies to your posts and questions from listeners are all real sources.

Do I need hundreds of questions?

No. Start with a handful per topic and expand as you find matches in your recordings.
